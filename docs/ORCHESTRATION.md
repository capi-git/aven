# Agent orchestration in Aven

Choose **Orchestrator** in the composer's add menu and describe the work. The lead proposes assignments before workers start. Review task descriptions, file scopes, dependencies, worker models, and the parallel worker count, then start the run. The default is two workers and the maximum is four. Available providers and models depend on what is installed and enabled in Aven.

Workers appear under their lead in the sidebar. **View agents** opens their transcripts beside the lead. The lead can inspect results, request corrections, answer worker questions and approvals, and redirect supported workers while they run. Overlapping file scopes wait their turn. Stopping the lead also stops its workers.

Run history is saved locally. An interrupted run reopens paused for review. Active or paused teams remain in their owning window; stop or finish the run before moving their tabs to another window.

## Message queue

Follow-ups default to **Queue** while an agent is working. Explicit **Steer** remains available for supported providers. Waiting messages retain their order, including when a provider switch is pending. **Resume** runs the next queued message. Rejected submissions keep the draft available to edit or retry.

## Discussions between two chats

Choose **Discuss** in the composer's add menu, beside Plan mode and Orchestrator, to have this chat talk your message through with another open chat or a new chat with the model you choose. A chip such as **Discuss with Codex · 3 rounds** appears in the message box; its dropdown picks the other chat (or **New chat with…** a model) and the rounds, one to six, and says why a chat cannot join or why sending would not start yet. Each round is one reply from each chat; the last rounds you chose are offered next time. Send the message to start: it becomes the topic. Typing `/discuss` turns the mode on too, keeping the rest of the message as the topic. Starting waits until both chats are idle with nothing waiting for you.

The discussion plays out in the chat that started it, and nothing else opens. Your topic shows as your own message, then **Round 1 of 3** and so on between rounds. That chat's own replies keep their usual look, work and all, under its pixel mascot and model name ("Claude · Opus 5.5") with a quiet rail in its provider's colour (Claude coral, Codex blue) beside them. The other chat's replies appear as its messages on the right, with its own mascot and name ("Codex · GPT-5.4"). A reply that agrees with the one before it gets a small check beside its name, and one that pushes back a small ↔ ("Agrees", "Pushes back"); Aven reads this from the reply's first words, without asking a model, and leaves anything mixed or unclear unmarked, and as such a reply lands its mascot nods or tilts its head once. While the other chat answers, its mascot types at the end of the chat. Long replies fold behind **Show more**. The other chat's last reply, which is never relayed back, is copied into the starting chat when the discussion ends, so that chat keeps the whole conversation. Everything outside a discussion looks as before, and the other chat's own transcript is unchanged.

A new partner chat gets no tab: it runs in the background, stays loaded while the discussion runs, is paused or still owes its closing note (and comes back after a reload), and is let go like any idle chat once the discussion is finished. It is listed in the sidebar under its project and opens from there like any chat. A chat you picked that is already open stays where it is. While the starting chat is on screen, the other chat's replies count as seen, so they do not notify you.

For the full side-by-side view, **Open discussion** in the bar above either chat's message box, on the wrap-up card, on the topic's card in the starting chat and on the relayed messages in the other chat, or pressing the connector in the sidebar, opens the discussion's page beside the starting chat. It shows the topic, each chat's finished replies as messages, the chat that opened the topic on the left and the one responding on the right, round dividers, the chat answering now typing, and the closing note; when all rounds complete, each side's last message gets one small hop. Pressing either side's name or mascot at the top of the page opens that chat. The page reads each reply from that chat's own transcript, the same text Aven relays, so nothing is stored twice. Its header has **Stop** and **Resume**.

While the discussion runs or is paused, the starting chat's own message box reads "Add something for both of them…" and its send button **Send to both**: what you send there goes to both chats instead of only to that chat's agent, and the bar above it still shows the discussion with **Stop**. The message box at the bottom of the page does the same. What you send shows as your own message and reaches both chats once: the chat answering next gets it with the reply it is passed ("The user adds: …"), and the other chat gets it with the following one, so neither turn is interrupted. Until it reaches the starting chat it waits at the end of that chat, below the other chat typing, marked "Goes to both with the next reply"; then it sits where that chat read it. While paused, sending resumes the discussion; during the last reply, or after the discussion has ended from the page's box, it continues for one more round, starting with your message. Once the discussion ends, the starting chat's message box goes back to messaging its agent. Each discussion keeps up to ten such messages with its record.

When a discussion uses up its rounds, a card after the closing note shows **Where they landed**: up to four things they agreed on and up to four still open. Aven writes it once, with the same cheapest model the memory keeper uses (Claude Haiku, else a small Codex model) and no tools, from a short summary of the thread that treats the agents' words as data, not instructions. It shows "Summing up…" meanwhile, and without a model, or if the call fails, the card has no lists. **Have Claude … build it** and **Have Codex … build it**, named by each side's provider and model, show that chat and send it, as your own message, a request to carry out the plan, quoting what they agreed on. The same card, smaller, appears in the starting chat's transcript right after its closing note, with the same buttons and an **Open discussion** link; the partner chat's transcript does not get it. It is only shown: the agent receives nothing new, since it already read every reply. Once written, the two lists are also kept in that closing note, so the chat still shows them as plain text after the discussion is no longer kept. A stopped discussion is not summed up; its page card has only the two buttons, and neither chat shows one. Closing the page stops nothing. Finished discussions are kept so their page can still be opened (the newest twenty per window); their replies stay in the two chats.

Aven brokers the conversation; neither agent gets a way to message the other, and an agent can never start one. The first chat receives the topic. Each time a chat finishes its turn, Aven sends that chat's final reply to the other one as a labelled message, shortened past 8,000 characters with a note. A relayed reply is passed as written: its `/` skills and `@` references are not expanded in the receiving chat. Relayed messages appear in the other chat's transcript with the sender's marks, and in the starting chat as the other chat's messages. When the rounds are used up, Aven posts a short note in both chats; where they landed is shown under the starting chat's note, but reaches an agent only when you press one of the build buttons.

A discussion pauses when either chat needs an approval or has a question, when a chat refuses or cannot take the message, and when the chat that should answer next is busy with your own turn, has your messages queued or has a card waiting in its message box. Your own work comes first; the relay never joins the queue or steers a running turn. Press **Resume** in the bar above either chat's message box or on the discussion's page to continue, or **Stop** to end it; a reply already in progress finishes but is not passed on. Stopping either chat's turn ends the discussion; an error pauses it, and Resume sends the same message again.

Discussions are saved locally and are driven by the window that started them. Reloading or closing that window pauses them. If a chat moves to another window, its bar there can stop the discussion, or resume it once both chats are open in that window.

## Access and review

Worker coordination does not replace the selected provider's permissions or your review of the result. Workers use the project folder and can make changes within the access granted to their processes. Review the assignment scopes and resulting Git diff, especially when several workers edit the same project.

The local control transport is authenticated, bound to loopback and the owning window, and scoped to the lead's run. Its credentials are passed to the lead process rather than shown in chat or supplied to worker processes.
