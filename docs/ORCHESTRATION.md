# Agent orchestration in Aven

Choose **Orchestrator** in the composer's add menu and describe the work. The lead proposes assignments before workers start. The plan card shows each task's instructions, its files as small chips ("Whole project" for `.`), what it waits for, its worker model, and the parallel worker count. Change a task's model, title or instructions, give its worker a **Role** (see "Roles" below; the lead never sets one), **Remove task** (tasks that waited for it no longer do; a plan keeps at least one task) or **Discard plan**, then **Confirm & start**. A task with a role shows it as a badge beside its title. The default is two workers and the maximum is four. Available providers and models depend on what is installed and enabled in Aven. Only the newest plan in a chat can start: an older card becomes read-only and reads "Replaced by a newer plan".

Workers appear under their lead in the sidebar. **Open agents** on the card or in the bar opens their transcripts beside the lead, and **Watch** opens the run in the team panel (see "Discussions, races and runs together" below). The lead can inspect results, request corrections, answer worker questions and approvals, and redirect supported workers while they run. Overlapping file scopes wait their turn.

Once you confirm, the plan card becomes the run's card, in the family's shape (see "Discussions, races and runs together" below): the lead's and each agent's mascot in its header, the plan's title with Running, Paused, Finished or Stopped, a status line, and a row per agent with its model, its state ("Working", "Lead deciding", "Accepted") and what it is doing. **Show plan** unfolds the plan it started from, read-only. When the run ends the card becomes its result: "Run finished · 4 agents", the lead's one-line verdict (the first sentence of its last reply), and a row per agent with its title, model, Accepted, Cancelled or Failed, and the files it touched, with **Review changes** (the Changes panel) and **Open agents**. A run that just finished also gets a line per agent on what it did, written once by the same cheapest model the memory keeper uses, from the end of each agent's result treated as data, not instructions; without a model, or if the call fails, the rows show only the model.

While a run is open, a slim bar inside the lead's message box says what it is doing: "Orchestrating · 2 working · 1 waiting", or "Paused" with the reason, with **Resume** (paused), **Stop run** and **Open agents**. A run ends only when the lead calls `finish`, and Aven wakes the lead only for new results or an agent waiting on it. When the lead is idle, no agent is working, no queued task can start and every result has reached it, the run would otherwise stay open; after a moment the run needs you: the bar says "The lead stopped without finishing" and offers **Finish run**, which ends the run as it is and cancels tasks that never started, and **Ask the lead to wrap up**, which sends the lead a short message of yours. The run posts one Activity entry for it ("Run needs you · The lead stopped without finishing"), marks the lead's chat in the sidebar and on Home, and counts toward the Dock badge until the lead works again or the run ends. **Stop run** is in the bar and on the sidebar card whenever the run is running or paused; it stops the lead and every agent. It is the lead chat's only Stop: the run's card, the run status and, while the lead works, the message box offer no second one. While a run is open, other chats in the project are refused with "<project> is busy with an orchestration in “<lead>”. Finish or stop it there." and their bar has **Go to lead**.

While its run is open, an agent's approval or question is its lead's to decide: the agent shows "Lead deciding" in a neutral colour, and there is no banner, Activity request, amber lead or Home "Needs you" row for it. The lead asks you in its own chat when the call is yours, and that counts as needing you as usual. If the lead was told about the request and then sits idle for 15 seconds without answering it, Aven hands that request to you: the agent's chat asks you as any chat does, and the run needs you there, with **Go to <agent>** in the lead's bar. Once a run finishes or stops, its agents are ordinary chats again: anything you ask one afterwards, and any approval or question it then raises, is yours, with a banner, an Activity entry, the lead marked in the sidebar, a Home "Needs you" row and the Dock badge. Agents' turns and the lead's behind-the-scenes turns post nothing to Activity; the run posts one entry, "Run finished · 4 agents" or "Run paused" with its reason, with a banner when you are elsewhere (Activity labels it Paused). A paused run says what happened and what to do next, such as "The lead checked in 20 times without finishing. Aven paused the run and stopped its agents. Check the files, then resume or stop the run."

The lead's chat stays loaded while its run is running or paused, so closing its tab never strands results or stops the run. Run history is saved locally. An interrupted run reopens paused for review. The run's card and sidebar card read Running, Paused, Finished or Stopped; a finished or stopped run's sidebar card folds to one line, such as "Finished · 4 agents · 2h ago", and opens when you click it, and its count leaves cancelled tasks out ("3 done · 1 cancelled"). Active or paused teams remain in their owning window; stop or finish the run before moving their tabs to another window.

## Message queue

Follow-ups default to **Queue** while an agent is working. Explicit **Steer** remains available for supported providers. Waiting messages retain their order, including when a provider switch is pending. **Resume** runs the next queued message. Rejected submissions keep the draft available to edit or retry.

## Discussions between two chats

Choose **Discuss** in the composer's add menu, beside Plan mode, Orchestrator and Race, to have this chat talk your message through with another open chat or a new chat with the model you choose. A chip such as **Discuss with Codex · 3 rounds** appears in the message box; its dropdown picks the other chat (or **New chat with…** a model), the rounds, one to six, and the other chat's **Role** (see "Roles" below), and says why a chat cannot join or why sending would not start yet. With a role the chip reads **Discuss with Codex as Skeptic · 3 rounds**. The role is for this discussion only. Each round is one reply from each chat; the last rounds you chose are offered next time. Send the message to start: it becomes the topic. Typing `/discuss` turns the mode on too, keeping the rest of the message as the topic. Starting waits until both chats are idle with nothing waiting for you.

The chat that started the discussion keeps it out of the way. Your topic shows as your own message, and everything after it folds into one live card: both agents' pixel mascots on tiles in their provider's colour (Claude coral, Codex blue), "Claude and Codex are discussing" with a **Round 2 of 4** badge, the latest reply on one line, and **Watch**; its Stop is the bar's. When the discussion needs you, the card turns amber with the reason and **Go to Codex** (the chat it waits for) or **Resume**. Once it is done it reads "Discussed for 4 rounds" with **Open**, followed by **Where they landed**; a stopped one reads "Discussion stopped after 2 rounds of 4". **Show in chat** unfolds the turns behind the card: that chat's own replies, the relayed messages with what you added, and, once it ended, the other chat's last reply on the right. The card redraws only when what it shows changes. Everything outside a discussion looks as before, and the other chat's own transcript is unchanged.

The conversation itself plays out in a floating panel inside the window. It opens by itself the first time a discussion starts; after that, a new discussion opens the way you last left the panel, open or shrunk. Its header reads "Discussion · “topic”" with shrink and close. Below it the two agents face each other, each with its mascot, model and part ("Opened the topic", "Responding", "Replying…"), the one speaking glowing, with "Round 2 of 4" and a track of the rounds between them. The thread shows each chat's finished replies as messages, the opening chat on the left and the responding one on the right, with round dividers and the chat answering now typing at the end. A reply that agrees with the one before it gets a small check beside its name, and one that pushes back a small ↔ ("Agrees", "Pushes back"); Aven reads this from the reply's first words, without asking a model, and leaves anything mixed or unclear unmarked, and as such a reply lands its mascot nods or tilts its head once. Long replies fold behind **Show more**. In a long discussion, rounds older than the last two fold behind "Rounds 1–3 · Show", a rail of round numbers jumps to any round, a "3 new since you looked" line marks where you left off, and only the rounds on show are drawn. The panel reads each reply from that chat's own transcript, the same text Aven relays, so nothing is stored twice; pressing either agent at the top opens that chat.

Drag the panel by its header and resize it from any edge. It stays inside the window and below the title bar, above panes and browser pages and below menus and dialogs, and its place, size and state are remembered for each window. Shrinking it (–) leaves a small pill with both mascots, the round and what is happening ("Codex is replying…", "Needs you" in amber, "Done"); press the pill to bring the panel back. Several discussions each get a pill, stacked, and the panel shows one at a time. A finished discussion's panel shows **Done** and stays until you close it, and closing it stops nothing. **Watch** or **Open** on the card, **Open discussion** in the bar in either chat's message box, on the wrap-up and on the relayed messages in the other chat, and the connector in the sidebar all open it again. On a Mac, ↗ opens the panel in a window of its own (see "The team panel" below). Discussion pages saved by an earlier version are dropped when the workspace is restored.

A new partner chat gets no tab: it runs in the background, stays loaded while the discussion runs, is paused, still owes its closing note or is open in the panel (and comes back after a reload), and is let go like any idle chat once the discussion is finished. It is listed in the sidebar under its project and opens from there like any chat. A chat you picked that is already open stays where it is. While the starting chat or the discussion's panel is on screen, the other chat's replies count as seen, so they do not notify you. The relayed turns post nothing to Activity of their own (they are recorded quietly, so each chat's status still reads right); the discussion posts one entry when it uses up its rounds, "Discussion ended · 4 rounds", on the starting chat, with a banner when you are elsewhere, and one, "Discussion paused" with its reason, each time it stops for you.

While the discussion runs or is paused, the starting chat's own message box reads "Add something for both of them…" and its send button **Send to both**: what you send there goes to both chats instead of only to that chat's agent, and the bar in it still shows the discussion ("Discussing · round 2 of 4 · with Codex · GPT-5") with **Stop**. The box at the bottom of the panel does the same. What you send shows as your own message in the panel and reaches both chats once: the chat answering next gets it with the reply it is passed ("The user adds: …"), and the other chat gets it with the following one, so neither turn is interrupted. Until it reaches the next chat it waits at the end of the panel, below the chat typing, marked "Goes to both with the next reply"; then it sits where that chat read it. In the starting chat, **Show in chat** shows it as "You added: …" with the relayed message that carried it. While paused, sending resumes the discussion; during the last reply, or after the discussion has ended from the panel's box, it continues for one more round, starting with your message. Once the discussion ends, the starting chat's message box goes back to messaging its agent. Each discussion keeps up to ten such messages with its record.

When a discussion uses up its rounds, a card after the panel's closing note shows **Where they landed**: up to four things they agreed on and up to four still open. Aven writes it once, with the same cheapest model the memory keeper uses (Claude Haiku, else a small Codex model) and no tools, from a short summary of the thread that treats the agents' words as data, not instructions. It shows "Summing up…" meanwhile, and without a model, or if the call fails, the card has no lists. **Have Claude … build it** and **Have Codex … build it**, named by each side's provider and model, show that chat and send it, as your own message, a request to carry out the plan, quoting what they agreed on. The same card, smaller, appears in the starting chat right after the discussion's card, with the same buttons and an **Open discussion** link; the partner chat's transcript does not get it. It is only shown: the agent receives nothing new, since it already read every reply. Once written, the two lists are also kept in that closing note, so the chat still shows them as plain text after the discussion is no longer kept. A stopped discussion is not summed up; its panel shows only the two buttons, and neither chat shows one. Finished discussions are kept, as a small record without the replies, for as long as the chat that started them exists, so their card and panel can always be opened again; their replies stay in the two chats. Deleting that chat forgets its discussions, and deleting the other chat stops a running one.

Aven brokers the conversation; neither agent gets a way to message the other, and an agent can never start one. The first chat receives the topic. Each time a chat finishes its turn, Aven sends that chat's final reply to the other one as a labelled message, shortened past 8,000 characters with a note. A relayed reply is passed as written: its `/` skills and `@` references are not expanded in the receiving chat. Relayed messages appear in the other chat's transcript with the sender's marks, and in the panel as the other chat's messages. When the rounds are used up, Aven posts a short note in both chats; where they landed is shown under the starting chat's note, but reaches an agent only when you press one of the build buttons.

A discussion pauses when either chat needs an approval or has a question, when a chat refuses or cannot take the message, and when the chat that should answer next is busy with your own turn, has your messages queued or has a card waiting in its message box. Your own work comes first; the relay never joins the queue or steers a running turn. Press **Resume** in the bar in either chat's message box, on the card or in the panel to continue (the bar offers **Go to <chat>** instead in a chat that is not the one it waits for), or **Stop** in the bar to end it; a reply already in progress finishes but is not passed on. Stopping either chat's turn ends the discussion; an error pauses it, and Resume sends the same message again.

Discussions are saved locally and are driven by the window that started them. Reloading or closing that window pauses them. If a chat moves to another window, its bar there can stop the discussion, or resume it once both chats are open in that window.

## Discussions, races and runs together

The three kinds of team work, a discussion, a race (see [RACE.md](RACE.md)) and an orchestration, share one way of showing themselves. Each feature keeps its own record; Aven reads all three as one small view of the team (its kind, the chat it started from, its members with their states and what they are doing, its status, what it needs from you and how it ended), so the bar, the cards and Activity never read transcripts.

- **The bar.** One slim bar in the message box of the chat a team started from and of its member chats: "Discussing · round 2 of 4 · with Codex · GPT-5", "Racing · 1 of 3 done" or "Orchestrating · 2 working · 1 waiting", with **Open discussion**, **Compare** or **Open agents**, **Resume** while paused, **Go to <chat>** when another chat is the one waiting on you, and one **Stop**: Stop in either discussion chat, **Stop run** for a lead, **Stop all** in the chat a race was sent from while an agent works. A race's own chats keep their own Stop, which stops that agent alone. Where the bar's Stop does what the chat's own would, the run status (and, for a lead, the message box) shows none.
- **The card.** In the chat the team started from: a header with the members' mascots, a title and a badge, a status line, a row per member, and actions at the bottom. A member brought in with a role has its first letter on its mascot and the role's name beside its name. A discussion's live card, a race's card after your message, and an orchestration's run card all take this shape, and each turns into its result when the team ends.
- **Needs you.** One rule everywhere (notifications, Activity, Home, the sidebar, the bar, the card and the Dock badge): a member's approval or question is yours, except an orchestration agent's while its run is open, which its lead decides unless it left it unanswered; a paused team, or a run whose lead stopped without finishing, needs you, with its reason and the chat to open; a race whose agents are all done is ready to compare, which is calm rather than amber, and one where none finished needs you.
- **Activity.** While a team runs, its members' turns (a discussion's relays, a race's agents, an orchestration's agents and the lead's behind-the-scenes turns) post nothing of their own. The team posts one entry for each new need and one at its end, "Discussion ended · 4 rounds", "Race ready to compare · 3 agents" or "Run finished · 4 agents", with a banner when you are elsewhere, and its end counts once for mascot cheers, the streak and the memory keeper. Members count as seen while the chat the team started from, the discussion panel or the race overview is on screen.
- **The team panel.** The discussion's floating panel watches races and runs too. **Watch** on a race's or a run's card, or in its bar, opens it; the panel and its pills behave as a discussion's (see above). A race shows one column per agent, side by side when the panel is wide and stacked when it is narrow: its mascot, model, state and clock, what it is doing, its lines and files changed, the first lines of its last reply once it stopped, and **Needs you · Go to chat** when it waits on you, with **Compare**, **Stop all** while an agent works and **Suggest a winner** and **Ask a reviewer** once every agent is done; a reviewer, once asked, shows below the columns with its **Reviewer** badge. Two models of one provider are named apart, as on the card. A run shows the lead's row (its state and what it is doing), then a row per agent: task (with its role's badge when it has one), model, state, what it is doing, what it waits for ("Waiting for review: …", "Waiting for files: …") or what its lead is deciding for it, and the files it touched, with **Open agents** and **Stop run**; a paused run, or one whose lead stopped without finishing, says why first, with **Resume** or **Finish run**. The header shows every member's mascot on its tile, the ones at work glowing, and a status line. The panel draws only the team's view, never a transcript (a discussion's replies are read as before), so a long run costs no more to watch than a short one. Every team in the window that is still open (running, waiting on you, or a race ready to compare) gets a pill of its own, stacked, amber when it needs you; closing a team's panel (×) hides its pill until you watch it again. Which teams the window watches, which one is open, which you closed, and where the panel sits are remembered for each window (`aven.teamPanel.<window>`).
- **Its own window.** On a Mac, ↗ in the panel's header opens the panel in a small window of its own that you can move anywhere, even to another screen. That window owns nothing: it draws what the window the team runs in sends it, and every button there (Stop, Resume, Finish run, Compare, Suggest a winner, Ask a reviewer, Open agents, Go to chat, what you add for both, Have … build it) is sent back to that window, which checks it against the team and does it. ↙ puts the panel back in that window, **Watch** there brings the small window forward, and closing the small window (or ⌘W in it) leaves the team there as a pill. ↙, **Go to chat**, **Compare**, **Open agents** and **Have … build it** bring that window forward, so what they show is never left out of sight. If you close the team's window while its chats still run (on a Mac it is then only hidden), the small window says "This team's window was closed. The team still runs there." with **Show it**, which brings it back; its buttons still work, and clicking Aven in the Dock brings the window back too. If the team's window goes for good, the small window says "This team's window was closed." and offers only Close. A reload of the team's window closes the small windows it had opened. The team's chats count as on screen while the small window has focus, so what they post to Activity is read there with no banner; while it doesn't, they count as out of sight, even with the team's window in front. Elsewhere the panel stays in its window and ↗ is not offered.
- **One row.** Home's "Running now" and Activity's "Running" show each team as one row with its mascots, its status and how many agents, opening the chat it started from. In the sidebar a running race is one row (its agents fold into the chat it came from); a discussion's two chats stay as your own rows, linked by their connector, and an orchestration's agents fold into the lead's card.

## Roles

An agent brought into a team can be given one of four fixed roles, each a short instruction in plain words, shown when you hover over it:

- **Reviewer**: "Review the work carefully: check that it does what was asked, look for bugs and risks, and say plainly what should change."
- **Tester**: "Work out how to prove it works: run or write the checks that matter, and report what passed, what failed and what is still untested."
- **Skeptic**: "Push back on weak points and look for what could go wrong; agree only when convinced."
- **Designer**: "Look at it from the user's side: how it looks, reads and feels to use. Suggest concrete changes to layout, wording and flow."

They are offered only where an agent is brought in: the other chat of a discussion (the Discuss chip's dropdown), an orchestration task's worker (its plan card) and a race's reviewer (**Ask a reviewer** on its card, always as Reviewer; see [RACE.md](RACE.md)). A race's own agents never take one, so their comparison stays fair, and the chat that starts a discussion or leads a run has none.

The instruction is added once, to the start of that agent's first message for the run, where its transcript shows it: "Your role in this discussion: Skeptic. Push back on weak points…" before the reply relayed to it, "Your role for this task: Tester. …" before a worker's instructions, "Your role for this race: Reviewer. …" before the comparison. Later relays, a lead's corrections and follow-ups go as they are. The role is kept with that discussion, run or race and shows as a badge on the agent's mascot and name: on the cards, in the discussion's bar, in the team panel and its pills, and on the plan card. Nothing remembers it for the next one.

## Access and review

Worker coordination does not replace the selected provider's permissions or your review of the result. Workers use the project folder and can make changes within the access granted to their processes. Review the assignment scopes and resulting Git diff, especially when several workers edit the same project.

The local control transport is authenticated, bound to loopback and the owning window, and scoped to the lead's run. Its credentials are passed to the lead process rather than shown in chat or supplied to worker processes.
